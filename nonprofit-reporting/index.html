The problem

The CRM migration fixed the database. It didn’t fix the reporting.

Nearly every nonprofit I talk to tells a version of the same story: donations, volunteers, and email lived in three disconnected places, so you moved everything onto one platform. That was the right call, and it worked.

Then the board asks for a quarterly picture, or a grant report comes due, and someone exports three CSVs and rebuilds the same summary by hand — again.

It happens because the questions your funders and your board ask are never quite the questions a CRM dashboard was built to answer. A grant wants this program’s outcomes over that period against a specific budget line. A board wants year-over-year retention next to volunteer hours next to the campaign that actually paid off. So the CRM holds the data, and the real reporting quietly moves back into a spreadsheet.

Your CRM is where the data lives. The spreadsheet is where the reporting actually happens. That spreadsheet deserves to be as good as the database you paid for.

Questions people ask

Nonprofit reporting, answered

We just moved to a new CRM. Isn’t this what it’s for?
Partly, and you should absolutely use its built-in reports where they fit. The gap shows up when a funder or board wants a specific cut — this program, this period, against this budget line, next to volunteer hours — that the standard dashboard doesn't produce. That's when someone exports to a spreadsheet and rebuilds it by hand. I make that spreadsheet clean and self-updating. Your CRM stays exactly as it is.
Do you work with Bloomerang, Neon, Givebutter, DonorPerfect?
Yes, and it doesn't much matter which. I work from the export your platform already produces — CSV or Excel — so I don't need access to your CRM at all. If you can download it, I can build on it. Salesforce NPSP, QuickBooks, and plain spreadsheets work the same way.

Yes, and it's one of the clearest wins. If each site keeps its own workbook and someone merges them by email every month, that merge is exactly the kind of thing that should happen automatically. Each location keeps entering data the way they already do, and the roll-up assembles itself.
